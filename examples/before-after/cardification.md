# Before → After: Cardification

**The failure:** every region enclosed in an identical card — hierarchy flattened by uniform containers.

## Before

```
┌──────┬──────┬──────┐
│ Card │ Card │ Card │
├──────┼──────┼──────┤
│ Card │ Card │ Card │
└──────┴──────┴──────┘
```

## Diagnosis

- **Focal check:** six zones at one weight → fails Rule 3 (`focal-point.md`). No first read.
- **Weight audit:** every card carries area + container + equal scale + equal contrast — four factors each, all equal. Weight ranking is a six-way tie.
- **Attention budget:** Primary ≈17%, everything else ≈17% each, containers consuming the remainder. Budget is flat by construction.
- **Root cause:** the layout was chosen before the content was ranked. "Grid of cards" is not a composition — it's the absence of one.

## Composition change

The team ranked the content first: one primary task region, one utility set, two supporting groups. Then redistributed weight — **not** by decorating the important card, but by *removing* enclosure from everything else.

## After

```
┌─────────────────────┬─────────┐
│                     │         │
│   PRIMARY CONTENT   │ Utility │
│                     │         │
├───────────┬─────────┴─────────┤
│ Secondary │ Supporting Data   │
└───────────┴───────────────────┘
```

Primary region: largest area + most breathing room + display type. Utility: compressed, minimal contrast. Secondary/supporting: open sections, hairline separation, reduced scale.

## Why it works

> The improvement does not come from decoration. It comes from redistributing visual weight and semantic hierarchy.

- The anchor now wins the squint test outright — it owns area, scale, *and* isolation simultaneously.
- Utility dropped from 17% to ~10% of attention while staying one gesture away (rail discipline).
- Three of the original six "cards" lost their containers entirely — and the page gained structure, because enclosure now means something again.

**Exercise:** take any six-card screen. Rank the six regions by decision value. You may keep at most two cards. Rebuild the rest with scale, space, and hairlines.
